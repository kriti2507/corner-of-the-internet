West of Sapporo, the Sea of Japan coast is the easiest day out from the city. Otaru is about 40 minutes away on the JR rapid train. Shakotan and Niseko are easier by car.

## Otaru

A port town with a restored canal, old stone warehouses, glass workshops, music box shops, and very good sushi. Go in the late afternoon and stay until the canal lamps come on. In February, the Snow Light Path fills the canal and the old Temiya railway line with candles (in 2027, from 6 to 13 February).

I've eaten at **Kaitenzushi Sakanaisshin** in Otaru. My note just says "Very nice kaiten sushi".

## Yoichi

A short drive or train ride past Otaru. It's home to Nikka's whisky distillery and a lot of fruit farms. In spring, I went to see the cherry blossoms at **[Akitaya Takahashi Farm](/places/akitaya-takahashi-farm)**: "Amazing sakura, and no people."

## Shakotan Peninsula

The coast at the end of the peninsula is known for its colour, called Shakotan blue. Walk out along the ridge path to Cape Kamui. From June to August it's sea urchin season, and the small restaurants near the cape serve uni bowls until they sell out, so arrive early.

## Niseko

A ski area around Mt Yōtei, about two hours from Sapporo. It's world-famous for powder snow from December to March. In summer it's quieter, with rafting, hiking and farm cafés.

## Good to know

Shakotan and Niseko are much easier with a car. In winter, many Shakotan restaurants close until spring.
