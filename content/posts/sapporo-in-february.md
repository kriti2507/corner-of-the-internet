February is when most people visit Sapporo, and it's still deep winter. Average highs are around 0°C, and lows are around −6°C. Days are a little longer and often brighter than in January.

## What's on

- **Sapporo Snow Festival.** The big one: huge snow sculptures along Odori Park, ice sculptures in Susukino, and snow slides at the Tsudome site. The 2027 festival runs from 4 to 11 February. Sculptures are lit up until 10pm.
- **Otaru Snow Light Path.** Candles and snow lanterns along the Otaru Canal and the old Temiya railway line, from 6 to 13 February 2027. It's quieter and more romantic than the Snow Festival. See [Otaru, Shakotan & Niseko](/blog/hokkaido-otaru-shakotan-niseko).
- **Chitose–Lake Shikotsu Ice Festival.** Until 23 February 2027.
- **Winter fireworks at Lake Tōya.** Short fireworks over the lake every night in early February. In 2026 they ran from 3 to 12 February. See [Lake Tōya, Noboribetsu & Shikotsu](/blog/hokkaido-lake-toya-noboribetsu-shikotsu).
- **The White Illumination** continues on Ekimae-dori until 11 February, and at the other city venues until mid-March.

## What to do

See the Snow Festival in the evening, when it's lit up, and again in daylight. Grab hot food from the stalls on Odori. Then get out of the crowds: ski for a day, soak in an [onsen](/blog/onsen-in-and-around-sapporo), or take the train to Otaru for the Snow Light Path.

## Around Hokkaido

February is peak drift ice season off Abashiri and Shiretoko, and the icebreaker cruises sell out. See [East Hokkaido](/blog/hokkaido-east-shiretoko-abashiri-akan). It's also peak powder season in [Niseko](/blog/hokkaido-otaru-shakotan-niseko).

## Good to know

Festival week is the most expensive and crowded time of the year in Sapporo. Book your hotel months ahead, or stay a few stops out on the subway.
