Hokkaido is about the size of Austria, and most first-time visitors try to see too much of it. Sapporo is a great base for day trips, but the east and the far north need a few days of their own. These are the areas I'd point a friend to, with the places I've been to in each.

## Close to Sapporo (day trips)

- **[Otaru, Shakotan & Niseko](/blog/hokkaido-otaru-shakotan-niseko).** A canal town 40 minutes away by train, the blue sea of Shakotan, and Niseko's powder snow.
- **[Lake Tōya, Noboribetsu & Shikotsu](/blog/hokkaido-lake-toya-noboribetsu-shikotsu).** Volcanic lakes and the most famous hot springs in Hokkaido, south of the city.

## One or two nights

- **[Furano, Biei & Tomamu](/blog/hokkaido-furano-biei-tomamu).** Lavender, rolling farmland, the Blue Pond, and a sea of clouds at dawn.
- **[Asahikawa & Daisetsuzan](/blog/hokkaido-asahikawa-daisetsuzan).** Asahiyama Zoo, Asahikawa ramen, and Hokkaido's highest mountains.
- **[Hakodate](/blog/hokkaido-hakodate).** A port city in the far south, with one of Japan's best night views and the star-shaped fort.

## Three days or more

- **[East Hokkaido](/blog/hokkaido-east-shiretoko-abashiri-akan).** Shiretoko's wild peninsula, drift ice off Abashiri, and the caldera lakes of Akan-Mashū.
- **[Wakkanai, Rishiri & Rebun](/blog/hokkaido-wakkanai-rishiri-rebun).** Japan's northern tip, and two islands covered in alpine flowers in early summer.

## Getting around

The JR limited express trains link Sapporo with Otaru, Asahikawa, Hakodate, Abashiri and Wakkanai. Asahikawa is about 1 hour 25 minutes away, and Hakodate about 3½ hours. For Biei, East Hokkaido and the Shakotan coast, a rental car makes life much easier. In winter, only drive if you're used to snow and ice.

## When to go

Summer (June to September) is easiest for driving and hiking. Winter is for skiing, festivals and drift ice. For the details month by month, see [when to visit Sapporo](/blog/sapporo-month-by-month).
