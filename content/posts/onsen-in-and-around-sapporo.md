Sapporo winters are long, and the city has plenty of places to warm up. These are the onsen I actually went to. All three take day visitors, so you can just turn up. Each name links to a short page with prices, hours and what to bring.

A few things apply everywhere. Most of these don't allow tattoos. Bring cash for the ticket machine, and some ¥100 coins for the lockers (you usually get them back).

## In the city

- **[Kura no Yu](/places/kura-no-yu-naebo)**, Naebo. One stop from Sapporo Station and about ¥500. Old-school and full of locals, with a big sauna and a rock bath outside. Open until midnight.
- **[Hana Yuzuki](/places/hana-yuzuki)**, Nijūyonken. A modern bathhouse with a bath for everything: massage jets, an electric bath, hot-water seats, saunas and three outdoor pools. You can eat and nap there afterwards.

## A short drive out

- **[Ban'ya no Yu](/places/banya-no-yu-ishikari)**, Ishikari. About 30 minutes north, where the Ishikari River meets the sea. The water is tea-brown "fossil seawater", used straight from the source. Go near sunset and watch from the rest area upstairs.

## The famous one

[Jōzankei](/places/jozankei-bridge) is the hot-spring town in a gorge about an hour south of the city by bus. I've only walked its bridge and gorge, so it's in the [outdoors guide](/blog/sapporo-outdoors-and-day-trips). It's worth a day of its own if you want a proper onsen town.

## Still on my list

Komichinoyu Honoka in Susukino, Tsukisamu Onsen, and Futomi Meisen Man'yō no Yu out toward Tōbetsu. I haven't made it to any of them yet.
