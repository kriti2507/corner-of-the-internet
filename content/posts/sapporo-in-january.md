January is the heart of winter. Average temperatures in Sapporo sit around −3°C, with daytime highs just below zero and lows around −6°C. The city is under snow, the skies are often grey, and it's beautiful.

## What's on

- **Sapporo White Illumination.** The Odori Park lights finish on 25 December, but the venues along Ekimae-dori, Minami 1-jo, Akapla and the Sapporo Station south plaza stay lit into the new year. In the 2026–27 season Ekimae-dori runs until 11 February and the others until 14 March.
- **SNOWFISH at Nakajima Park.** New for 2026–27: a light installation by the Australian art group ENESS, from 9 to 28 January 2027.
- **Hatsumōde.** The first shrine visit of the year. Hokkaido Shrine in Maruyama is the big one, and the queues in the first days of January are long.
- **Chitose–Lake Shikotsu Ice Festival.** Ice sculptures lit up at night by the lake, from 30 January to 23 February 2027.

The snow lanterns at Jōzankei (Yukitōro) held their final edition in January 2026, so they won't be back.

## What to do

Go skiing. Sapporo Teine and Sapporo Kokusai are under an hour from the city, and Bankei is about 20 minutes away. Warm up in an [onsen](/blog/onsen-in-and-around-sapporo) afterwards. On the coldest days, Sapporo's underground walkways let you get from the station to Susukino without going outside.

## Around Hokkaido

Drift ice usually reaches the Okhotsk coast in late January, and the icebreaker cruises from Abashiri start around then. See [East Hokkaido](/blog/hokkaido-east-shiretoko-abashiri-akan). The penguin walk at Asahiyama Zoo is on through the winter too. See [Asahikawa & Daisetsuzan](/blog/hokkaido-asahikawa-daisetsuzan).

## Good to know

Many shops and restaurants close for the first few days of January. Pack proper winter boots: the pavements are icy, and everyone falls at least once.
