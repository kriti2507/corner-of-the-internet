South of Sapporo is volcano country: caldera lakes, active volcanoes, and some of Hokkaido's best-known hot springs. It's easy to cover in a long day by car, or one night in an onsen town.

## Lake Tōya

A round caldera lake with an island in the middle and an active volcano, Mt Usu, on its southern shore. From late April to the end of October there are fireworks over the lake every night at 8:45pm (in 2026, from 28 April to 31 October). You can watch from the lakeside or from a boat. By train, it's about 2 hours from Sapporo to Tōya Station, then a short bus ride.

Places I've been to around the lake:

- **Lake Hill Farm.** A dairy farm with gelato. My note: "Nice cheese, good view on a sunny day, and goats."
- **Café Cohum.** My note: "Good berry cake."
- **Ukimidō,** a little hall by the lake.

On the road between Tōya and Sapporo, I've also stayed at **Midorinokaze Resort Kitayuzawa** and visited **Hyakujōjiki Cave** in Ōtaki.

## Noboribetsu

Hokkaido's most famous onsen town, built around Jigokudani ("Hell Valley"), a crater full of steaming vents and sulphur streams. Walk the valley, then soak in one of the big hotel baths. It's about 1 hour 15 minutes from Sapporo by limited express.

## Lake Shikotsu

A deep, clear lake close to New Chitose Airport. In winter, the Chitose–Lake Shikotsu Ice Festival builds ice sculptures from lake water and lights them up at night (in 2027, from 30 January to 23 February).

## Good to know

Tōya also has a short winter fireworks season in early February. If you only have one night, stay in Tōya in summer and in Noboribetsu in winter.
