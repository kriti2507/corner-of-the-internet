I lived in Sapporo before moving to Tokyo in June 2026. These are the coffee places there that I actually went to, grouped by what you might be in the mood for. Lines in quotes are my own notes from Google Maps. The rest is what each place is known for and what other visitors tend to say. Each name links to a short page with the details.

Sapporo takes coffee seriously. The old local style is a dark roast brewed through flannel, called nel drip. The newer specialty shops lean lighter, and most of my list is the newer kind.

## If you want a really good cup

- **[NODE](/places/node-coffee)**, Odori. A long, rotating list of single origins, each brewed as a pour-over when you order. Not a rush stop.
- **[Marumi Coffee, Odori Park](/places/marumi-coffee-odori)**. "Very good coffee." A Sapporo roaster whose owner is a Q Grader, with a deep bean list.
- **[Marumi Coffee Stand, Nakajima Park](/places/marumi-coffee-nakajima-park)**. The same beans with proper seats and desserts, next to the park.
- **[ippo ippo](/places/ippo-ippo)**, near the Jōgai market. An owner-run stand. Ask him which single origin to get. Closes mid-afternoon.
- **[Coffee-ya-san Tsukisai](/places/tsukisai-coffee)**, Kita 12-jo. A quiet roaster with a Japanese feel and Japanese sweets. Nel drip in winter.
- **[Rain coffee stand](/places/rain-coffee-stand)**, Tanukikoji. A local roaster's stand, with tasting cards for every bean and plant milks done well.

## If you want a latte

- **[Standard Coffee Lab.](/places/standard-coffee-lab)**, Tanukikoji. "White chocolate matcha latte with beautiful latte art." Cash only.
- **[BARISTART COFFEE](/places/baristart-coffee)**, Tanukikoji. "Good coffee." You pick the beans and the Hokkaido milk. Mostly takeaway.
- **[sal coffee](/places/sal-coffee)**, Tanukikoji. "Really good latte." Flavoured lattes and Basque cheesecake.

## If you want espresso, Australian style

- **[Love espresso](/places/love-espresso)**, Tanukikoji. A tiny bar for a strong, rich shot.
- **[Postman Oak](/places/postman-oak)**, Nakajima Park. Piccolos, banana bread, and a dog called Oak at the door.
- **[poool](/places/poool-sapporo)**, Sapporo Station. Flat whites and a lot of room to work.

## If you need to sit and work

- **[poool](/places/poool-sapporo)**, Sapporo Station. Wifi, power, and bookshelves, open into the evening.
- **[Ueshima Coffee, Sapporo APIA](/places/ueshima-coffee-apia)**, under the station. "Good coffee, and free wifi."
- **[POP/iN](/places/pop-in)**, Nishi 18-chome. "Very nice coffee and bagels." Opens at 8am.

## If you want a view

- **[Mingus Coffee](/places/mingus-coffee)**, Odori. "Good outdoor view." A 7th-floor jazz café with a terrace facing the TV Tower, open until midnight.

## The Starbucks ones

- **[Starbucks, Sapporo PARCO](/places/starbucks-sapporo-parco)**, Odori. Hokkaido's first Starbucks, and where I studied Japanese almost every day after work.
- **[Starbucks, Sapporo Asahigaoka](/places/starbucks-sapporo-asahigaoka)**. A drive-through store that was one of the first outside Tokyo to get nitro cold brew. Ask before you go.

## Still on my list

Morihico, the original shop of Sapporo's biggest coffee brand, and Tokumitsu Coffee on Odori. I haven't made it to either yet.
